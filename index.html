Internet: what the readings mean

The online signal is the browser's own hint and can be wrong. Effective type, round-trip estimate, downlink estimate, and data-saver state come from the optional Network Information API; many browsers do not expose it. The test sends small requests to Google, Cloudflare, Mozilla, and Wikipedia; choose one or three rounds to compare average, minimum, and maximum request times. A completed no-cors request is opaque: browser privacy rules hide its HTTP status and contents, so it is only a rough reachability signal. This is not a bandwidth speed test, DNS diagnosis, Wi-Fi signal reading, or proof that every site works.

Bluetooth: discovery, connection, and services

“Available” means the browser reports a usable Bluetooth adapter; it does not mean a device is paired or connected. The device chooser is controlled by the browser and operating system. Once you select a device, this page attempts a GATT connection and reads battery level only if the device exposes the standard Battery service. The browser grants access only to the requested Battery and Device Information services; it does not show nearby devices, pairing keys, signal strength, or OS-wide connection history. Disconnecting here ends this page's GATT session.

The local P2P test creates two temporary peers in this page only. It does not scan for or contact outside peers.

What the GATT explorer can see

Services group characteristics. Characteristics advertise read, write, notify, and indicate properties; descriptors provide metadata or configuration. The explorer enumerates granted services, lists their characteristics and descriptors, and reads only fields marked readable. It does not write. The returned bytes are raw values; profile-specific formats may need decoding to turn them into measurements.

Why this cannot show everything from every device

Web Bluetooth requires a user gesture and a browser chooser. The browser cannot silently scan all devices or expose every paired device, MAC address, pairing key, or OS connection. Standard services and optional vendor UUIDs can be requested before connecting, but the device must expose them. Advertisement monitoring may provide RSSI and broadcast data in supported browsers; this is not a general radio scanner. Classic Bluetooth audio/serial data is outside the browser API.

A note on visibility

“Online” reflects the browser's network hint; it does not guarantee that a website or service is reachable. Bluetooth device selection is initiated by you and depends on browser and operating-system support.

To change Wi-Fi, Bluetooth, firewall, or sharing settings, use your computer's system settings. A local HTML page cannot safely control those OS services.
